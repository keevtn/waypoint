# Automobile Insurance Guide

## Required coverages in Pennsylvania

Every vehicle registered in Pennsylvania must carry three coverages. Medical benefits coverage pays the medical bills for you and others who are covered by your policy; the minimum limit is $5,000, and higher limits are available. Bodily injury liability pays for injuries you cause to other people; the minimum limit is $15,000/$30,000, where $15,000 pays for injuries to one person and $30,000 is the total available for one accident. Property damage liability pays for damage you cause to another person's property; the minimum limit is $5,000.

## Limited tort and full tort

Pennsylvania drivers choose a tort option, which controls your right to sue another driver after an accident. Limited tort offers you significant savings on your premium, but you are not able to recover certain damages, such as payments for pain and suffering, unless your injuries meet one of the exceptions in the law. Under limited tort you can still recover your medical bills and other out-of-pocket expenses. Full tort gives you unrestricted rights to seek compensation, including for pain and suffering, and costs more. If you fail to specially elect limited tort, you will be deemed to have elected full tort.

## Uninsured and underinsured motorist coverage

Uninsured motorist (UM) coverage protects you and members of your household if you are injured by an at-fault driver who has no insurance or by a hit-and-run driver. Underinsured motorist (UIM) coverage applies when the at-fault driver's liability limits are too low to pay for your injuries. UM and UIM pay for injuries, not for property damage. Insurers must offer UM and UIM coverage; you may reject them only in writing.

## Stacking

Stacking UM/UIM coverage multiplies the per-vehicle limit by the number of vehicles on your policy. Stacked coverage applies unless you reject it in writing by signing a rejection form. Rejecting stacking lowers your premium.

## Collision and comprehensive

Collision coverage pays for damage to your own car from hitting another vehicle or an object. Comprehensive coverage pays for damage to your car from causes other than a collision, such as theft, fire, vandalism, or weather. Both are optional under state law and are subject to a deductible you choose. It may not be cost-effective to carry comprehensive or collision coverage on a car worth less than $1,000. If your car is financed or leased, your lender or leasing company will usually require both.

## Ways to manage your premium

You may be able to reduce optional limits or increase your deductibles while still carrying enough insurance. Compare the same coverages, limits, and deductibles across companies, and ask about discounts you may qualify for.
