# Auto Insurance When You Finance a Car

## Lenders require physical damage coverage

When you finance a car, the car is collateral for the loan. Auto finance contracts generally require you to maintain comprehensive and collision insurance that covers physical damage to the vehicle, to protect the value of the collateral. State minimum liability coverage alone does not meet this requirement. Read your loan contract to see the exact coverages and maximum deductible your lender requires, and ask the lender to confirm them.

## Proof of insurance and listing the lender

Your lender will usually ask you to list it on your policy as the lienholder or loss payee and to send proof of coverage. Send proof of coverage promptly when you buy or change a policy.

## Force-placed insurance

If your coverage lapses or does not meet the contract's requirements, the contract usually gives the lender the right to buy insurance on the vehicle and charge you for it. This is called force-placed or collateral protection insurance. It protects only the lender, not you; it generally covers only physical damage to or loss of the vehicle up to the loan balance, with no liability or medical coverage. It is usually much more expensive than a policy you could buy yourself.

## If you are charged for force-placed insurance

If you already have your own policy, send the lender proof of coverage and ask it to cancel the force-placed policy and refund premiums for any overlapping period.
