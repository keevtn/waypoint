# Housing Affordability and Cost Burden

## The 30 percent standard

Since 1981, standard practice of federal, state, and local governments has been to judge housing affordable if a household pays no more than 30 percent of its income for gross housing costs, including rent and utilities. Households paying more than 30 percent are considered cost burdened, and households paying more than 50 percent are considered severely cost burdened.

## Where the standard came from

The benchmark comes from rent limits in federally subsidized housing. The maximum rent was set at 20 percent of income in the 1940s, rose to 25 percent in 1969, and to 30 percent in 1981.

## Limits of the ratio

The 30 percent ratio is a rule of thumb. It does not account for household size or local cost of living, so two households spending the same share of income on housing can face very different strain. A residual income approach, which looks at how much money a household has left after paying for housing and other necessary expenses, can be a more useful measure.
