# A Consumer's Guide to Auto Insurance

## Liability coverage

Most states require drivers to carry a minimum amount of liability coverage. Bodily injury liability helps pay for other people's medical costs, and property damage liability helps pay for repairs to other people's property, when you are at fault. You will be responsible for any property damage or bodily injury you cause above your limits, so think carefully before buying only the minimum.

## Collision and comprehensive coverage

Collision coverage pays for damage to your vehicle when it hits another car or an object. Comprehensive coverage pays for losses that are not collisions, such as fire, theft, or weather damage. Collision and comprehensive coverage are not required by state law, but lenders may require them for financed or leased vehicles.

## There is no such thing as "full coverage"

"Full coverage" is not a type of policy. People often use the phrase to mean liability plus collision and comprehensive, but every policy has limits and exclusions. Ask exactly which coverages, limits, and deductibles a quote includes.

## Deciding whether to keep physical damage coverage

Before dropping comprehensive or collision coverage, weigh the value of the car and what it would cost to repair or replace it against what you would save in premium.

## Review your policy

Regularly review your auto policy and make updates when your circumstances change, such as buying a car, moving, or adding a driver.
